    How to read this, and where it can mislead

    • Supply is the 12-month average of pounds distributed each month by active food shelves, from your organization export, added up by the county each food shelf sits in. Need is the Census Bureau's 2024 estimate of residents below the poverty line (SAIPE, released January 2026). Median household income is from the same source.
    • Pounds are used instead of a count of food shelves because counts reward having many small sites. Rural counties look well served by count and poorly served by volume.
    • People cross county lines for food. A large food shelf near a border (Oakdale, for example) lifts its own county and hides what it does for the neighbors. Wilkin County shows no food shelves at all; residents there likely use ones in neighboring counties or across the river in North Dakota.
    • Food shelves only. Tribal food programs, meal programs, mobile distributions and farmers markets in your export are not counted, which understates supply in counties that lean on them.
    • Poverty rates in college towns (Winona, Blue Earth, Stevens, Clay) are inflated by students, and the poverty line is a stricter test than food shelf eligibility. Treat the list as places to ask questions, not as a ranking.
    • Dots sit at the centre of each food shelf's census block, not its street address.
